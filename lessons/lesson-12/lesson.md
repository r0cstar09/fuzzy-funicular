# Spanish Daily Lesson 12 (Writing-First)

## 1) cognitive_shift

- **English trap:** Treating “to be” as one universal verb and forgetting adjective agreement.
- **Spanish logic:** Use `estar` for current state/condition (emotion, health, temporary status). Match adjective gender and number with the subject.
- **target pattern/formula:**
  - `Sujeto + estar + adjetivo de estado`
  - `¿Cómo estás?` + `Estoy ...`
  - `¿Por qué estás ...?` + `Porque ...`

### 5 natural examples
1. Estoy cansado porque tengo que trabajar.
2. María está preocupada hoy.
3. ¿Por qué están nerviosos ellos?
4. Las mujeres están contentas.
5. La sopa está caliente.

## 3) pattern_mutation

Transform each base sentence as instructed.

1. Base: `Estoy cansado.` -> change to feminine speaker.
2. Base: `María está enferma.` -> turn into question.
3. Base: `Ellos están nerviosos.` -> change to feminine plural.
4. Base: `Estoy triste porque tengo que trabajar.` -> change reason to `no puedo dormir`.
5. Base: `El café está caliente.` -> make it negative.
6. Base: `La cerveza está fría.` -> change to plural subject `las cervezas`.
7. Base: `¿Cómo estás?` -> answer with `confundido` (male speaker).
8. Base: `Estoy enojada.` -> ask why.
9. Base: `Los tacos están deliciosos.` -> change to singular `el taco`.
10. Base: `Nosotros estamos preocupados.` -> change to `nosotras`.
11. Base: `¿Por qué estás triste?` -> answer with `porque no puedo hablar con mi amiga`.
12. Base: `La fruta está fría.` -> change to `las frutas`.
13. Base: `Juan está contento.` -> make it negative.
14. Base: `Estoy feliz.` -> add reason with `porque voy a descansar`.
15. Base: `Las mujeres están cansadas.` -> change to masculine plural `los hombres`.

## 4) contrastive_discrimination

Choose the best option and write in Spanish.

1. “I am tired (right now).” (`estoy` vs `soy`)
2. “I am a patient person.” (`soy` vs `estoy`)
3. “María is sick today.” (`está enferma` vs `es enferma`)
4. “The tacos are delicious.” (`están deliciosos` vs `son deliciosos`) current tasting context.
5. “How are you?” (`¿cómo estás?` vs `¿cómo eres?`)
6. “What are you like?” (`¿cómo eres?` vs `¿cómo estás?`)
7. “The coffee is hot.” (`está caliente` vs `es caliente`) served-now context.
8. “They are worried because they have a test.” (`están preocupados` vs `son preocupados`)
9. “I am happy today.” (`estoy feliz` vs `soy feliz`) temporary state emphasis.
10. “The beer is cold.” (`está fría` vs `es fría`) serving condition.

## 7) common_errors

1. **Using `ser` for temporary emotions**  
   Why: English has only one “to be.”  
   Correct: `Estoy cansado.`
2. **No adjective agreement in gender**  
   Why: English adjectives do not change.  
   Correct: `María está cansada.`
3. **No adjective agreement in number**  
   Why: Learner keeps adjective singular.  
   Correct: `Ellos están preocupados.`
4. **Using `es enfermo` for being sick now**  
   Why: Confusing trait vs condition.  
   Correct: `Está enfermo hoy.`
5. **Saying `soy bien` for “I’m good”**  
   Why: Literal translation.  
   Correct: `Estoy bien.`
6. **Using `ser` for food condition now**  
   Why: English uses “is” for both.  
   Correct: `La sopa está caliente.`
7. **Missing `porque` clause connector**  
   Why: Direct sentence stacking.  
   Correct: `Estoy triste porque no puedo dormir.`
8. **Dropping accent in `estás`**  
   Why: Fast typing.  
   Correct: `¿Cómo estás?`
9. **Using infinitive after `estar`**  
   Why: Pattern confusion with modal + infinitive lessons.  
   Correct: `Estoy nervioso`, not `estoy nerviosar`.
10. **Using wrong regional adjective without awareness**  
    Why: Mixing `enojado` / `enfadado` inconsistently.  
    Correct: both valid; use one consistently in context.
