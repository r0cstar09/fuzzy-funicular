# Spanish Daily Lesson 46: Antes de que vs Antes de

## 1) cognitive_shift

**English trap**  
Using one English-style "before + verb" structure for every sentence.

**Spanish logic**  
Spanish splits this into two patterns:
- `antes de que + subjuntivo` when the subject changes  
- `antes de + infinitivo` when the subject stays the same

**target pattern / formula**  
- subject change: `antes de que + present subjunctive`  
- same subject: `antes de + infinitive`  
- place `antes de (que)` at sentence start or middle without changing rule  
- common subjunctive forms in this frame: `llegue`, `empiece`, `salga`, `venga`, `haga`

**5 natural examples**  
1. Debo terminar esto antes de que llegue mi jefe.  
2. Voy a salir antes de que tu llegues.  
3. Suelo leer antes de acostarme.  
4. Quiero organizar todo antes de irme de vacaciones.  
5. Llamame antes de que tomes una decision.

4. Base: Quiero organizar todo antes de irme de vacaciones. -> change to plural `nosotros`.

6. Base: Compramos comida antes de que lleguen los invitados. -> make it future with `vamos a`.  
7. Base: Ella limpia antes de que sus padres vengan. -> change subject to `ellos` for second clause.  
8. Base: Hago ejercicio antes de salir al trabajo. -> change to first person plural.

11. Base: Me ducho antes de acostarme. -> change reflexive infinitive to `vestirme`.

1. I read before sleeping. (`Leo antes de dormir.` vs `Leo antes de que duermo.`)

5. She drinks coffee before leaving the house. (`Ella bebe cafe antes de salir de casa.` vs `Ella bebe cafe antes de que salga de casa.` with same subject)  
6. We should eat before the guests arrive. (`Debemos comer antes de que lleguen los invitados.` vs `Debemos comer antes de llegar los invitados.`)  
7. I organize everything before going on vacation. (`Organizo todo antes de irme de vacaciones.` vs `Organizo todo antes de que me voy de vacaciones.`)

## 7) common_errors

1. **Mistake:** `antes de que + indicative`  
   **Why it happens:** direct transfer from English present tense.  
   **Correct Spanish:** use subjunctive after `antes de que`.

2. **Mistake:** `antes de que` when subject does not change  
   **Why it happens:** overgeneralizing trigger phrase.  
   **Correct Spanish:** use `antes de + infinitive` with same subject.

3. **Mistake:** `antes de + conjugated verb`  
   **Why it happens:** mixing both structures.  
   **Correct Spanish:** infinitive only after `antes de`.

4. **Mistake:** dropping `que` in subject-change sentences  
   **Why it happens:** trying to shorten sentence.  
   **Correct Spanish:** keep `antes de que + subjuntivo`.

5. **Mistake:** wrong subjunctive form (`llega` instead of `llegue`)  
   **Why it happens:** indicative default.  
   **Correct Spanish:** use correct present subjunctive conjugation.

6. **Mistake:** reflexive infinitive not adjusted (`antes de acostar`)  
   **Why it happens:** forgetting pronoun in infinitive pattern.  
   **Correct Spanish:** `antes de acostarme/acostarte`.

7. **Mistake:** literal word order from English  
   **Why it happens:** direct translation without Spanish flow.  
   **Correct Spanish:** place `antes de (que)` naturally at start or middle.

8. **Mistake:** ignoring accent marks in subjunctive spellings  
   **Why it happens:** fast writing.  
   **Correct Spanish:** maintain standard accents where needed.

9. **Mistake:** using future after `antes de que`  
   **Why it happens:** event is future in meaning.  
   **Correct Spanish:** still use present subjunctive (`antes de que llegue`).

10. **Mistake:** choosing infinitive in interruption-like event clauses  
    **Why it happens:** not checking subject change.  
    **Correct Spanish:** if subjects differ, use `antes de que + subjuntivo`.
