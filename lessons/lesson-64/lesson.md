# Spanish Daily Lesson 64: Soler + Infinitive

## 1) cognitive_shift

**English trap**  
Overusing adverbs like "usually" and long past routines with many fully conjugated verbs.

**Spanish logic**  
Spanish uses `soler + infinitive` as a compact routine pattern.  
Present `soler` = what you usually do now.  
Imperfect `soler` = what you used to do before.

**target pattern / formula**  
- present routine: `suelo/sueles/suele/solemos/suelen + infinitive`
- past routine: `solia/solias/solia/soliamos/solian + infinitive`
- keep main action as infinitive
- subject pronoun often omitted in present
- in imperfect, add subject when meaning could be ambiguous

**5 natural examples**  
1. Suelo desayunar temprano.  
2. Mi hermano suele levantarse a las seis.  
3. Solemos cocinar en casa.  
4. Solia leer todas las noches.  
5. Mis padres solian viajar mucho.

## 3) pattern_mutation

Transform each base sentence as instructed.

1. Base: Suelo fumar. -> change infinitive to `correr`.  
2. Base: Mi hermano suele levantarse temprano. -> change subject to plural `mis hermanos`.  
3. Base: Solemos cocinar en casa. -> change infinitive to `comer`.  
4. Base: Solia leer todas las noches. -> change infinitive to `escribir`.  
5. Base: Juan solia tocar la guitarra. -> change instrument to `el piano`.  
6. Base: Mis padres solian viajar mucho. -> change adverb to `poco`.  
7. Base: Sueles tomar cafe por la manana. -> change drink to `te`.  
8. Base: Ella suele estudiar antes de dormir. -> change infinitive to `meditar`.  
9. Base: Soliamos ver esa serie juntos. -> change noun to `esa pelicula`.  
10. Base: El suele olvidar sus llaves. -> change object to `su cartera`.  
11. Base: Yo solia cocinar con mi abuela. -> change person to `nosotros`.  
12. Base: Ustedes suelen terminar tarde. -> change adverb to `temprano`.  
13. Base: Mis amigos solian venir los domingos. -> change day to `los viernes`.  
14. Base: Suelo escuchar podcasts mientras conduzco. -> change infinitive to `leer noticias`.  
15. Base: Mi jefe solia llegar muy temprano. -> change tense to present habitual.

## 4) contrastive_discrimination

1. I usually read at night. (`Suelo leer por la noche.` vs `Estoy leyendo por la noche.`)  
2. We used to travel a lot. (`Soliamos viajar mucho.` vs `Viajabamos mucho ahora.`)  
3. She usually studies before bed. (`Suele estudiar antes de dormir.` vs `Suele estudia antes de dormir.`)

## 7) common_errors

1. **Mistake:** conjugating both verbs after `soler`  
   **Why it happens:** English-style two-verb pressure.  
   **Correct Spanish:** after `soler`, keep infinitive.

2. **Mistake:** using present `soler` for past routines  
   **Why it happens:** not distinguishing timeline.  
   **Correct Spanish:** use imperfect forms for used-to meaning.

3. **Mistake:** avoiding `soler` and overusing `normalmente`  
   **Why it happens:** adverb-first habit.  
   **Correct Spanish:** use `soler` as a core routine structure.

4. **Mistake:** saying `soler de` with extra preposition  
   **Why it happens:** analogies with other verb chunks.  
   **Correct Spanish:** `soler` takes direct infinitive, no preposition.

5. **Mistake:** wrong forms like `soles` or `solen`  
   **Why it happens:** pattern regularization.  
   **Correct Spanish:** memorize irregular stem and accents.

6. **Mistake:** forcing subject pronouns in every sentence  
   **Why it happens:** English subject habit.  
   **Correct Spanish:** omit pronoun unless needed for clarity/emphasis.

7. **Mistake:** not clarifying subject in ambiguous imperfect  
   **Why it happens:** `solia` can map to multiple subjects.  
   **Correct Spanish:** include subject when context is unclear.

8. **Mistake:** mixing habitual and one-time contexts  
   **Why it happens:** event vs routine not separated.  
   **Correct Spanish:** use `soler` for repeated patterns.

9. **Mistake:** adding progressive when habitual is intended  
   **Why it happens:** confusion with current action forms.  
   **Correct Spanish:** `soler` expresses routine, not ongoing event.

10. **Mistake:** treating `soler` as advanced and avoiding it  
    **Why it happens:** low confidence with irregular verbs.  
    **Correct Spanish:** practice chunk templates to automate usage.
