# Spanish Daily Lesson 77: Accidental Se (Olvidársele, Caérsele, Rompérsele, Perdérsele, Acabársele)

## 1) cognitive_shift

**English trap**  
Using I forgot / I dropped / I broke with the person as subject, as if the event were fully intentional.

**Spanish logic**  
Spanish often externalizes the event: the thing happens to you. Se marks unplanned occurrence, the IO pronoun marks who is affected, and the lost/broken/forgotten item is the subject.

**target pattern / formula**  
- forget: `Se me olvido / Se me olvidaron + thing + (place)`  
- drop: `Se me cayo / Se me cayeron + thing`  
- break: `Se me rompio / Se nos rompieron + thing`  
- lose/misplace: `Se le perdio / Se me perdieron + thing + (place)`  
- run out: `Se me acabo / Se nos acabaron + thing`  
- optional clarifier: `A Maria se le perdio el pasaporte.`

**5 natural examples**  
1. Se me olvido el celular en casa.  
2. Se le cayo la tarjeta de credito.  
3. Se nos rompio la ventana.  
4. A Maria se le perdio el pasaporte en el aeropuerto.  
5. Se nos acabaron las excusas.

## 4) contrastive_discrimination

Choose the natural Spanish sentence for unplanned/affected-party framing.

1. I forgot my phone at home. (`Olvide mi celular en casa.` vs `Se me olvido el celular en casa.`)  
2. I dropped the card. (`Cai la tarjeta.` vs `Se me cayo la tarjeta.`)  
3. The window broke on us. (`Rompimos la ventana.` vs `Se nos rompio la ventana.`)  
4. She lost her passport. (`Perdio su pasaporte.` vs `Se le perdio el pasaporte.`)  
5. We ran out of time. (`Acabamos el tiempo.` vs `Se nos acabo el tiempo.`)  
6. I forgot the keys (plural). (`Se me olvido las llaves.` vs `Se me olvidaron las llaves.`)  
7. They dropped the cameras. (`Se les cayo las camaras.` vs `Se les cayeron las camaras.`)  
8. I fell down the stairs. (`Se me cai en las escaleras.` vs `Me cai en las escaleras.`)

## 7) common_errors

1. **Mistake:** `Olvide mi celular en casa`  
   **Why it happens:** English puts the person as intentional subject instead of accidental se frame.  
   **Correct Spanish:** `Se me olvido el celular en casa.`

2. **Mistake:** `Se me olvido las llaves`  
   **Why it happens:** Plural subject does not trigger plural verb agreement.  
   **Correct Spanish:** `Se me olvidaron las llaves.`

3. **Mistake:** `Se me cai la tarjeta`  
   **Why it happens:** Caerse reflexive for person falling is mixed with thing dropping.  
   **Correct Spanish:** `Se me cayo la tarjeta.`

4. **Mistake:** `Rompimos la ventana`  
   **Why it happens:** Active we-broke framing is used for an unplanned break.  
   **Correct Spanish:** `Se nos rompio la ventana.`

5. **Mistake:** `Perdio su pasaporte`  
   **Why it happens:** Simple transitive lose replaces affected-party se pattern.  
   **Correct Spanish:** `Se le perdio el pasaporte.`

6. **Mistake:** `Se les cayo las camaras`  
   **Why it happens:** Singular verb with plural subject.  
   **Correct Spanish:** `Se les cayeron las camaras.`

7. **Mistake:** `Se me olvido mi celular`  
   **Why it happens:** Possessive is added even though affected item is already the subject.  
   **Correct Spanish:** `Se me olvido el celular.`

8. **Mistake:** `A Maria le perdio el pasaporte`  
   **Why it happens:** Accidental se is omitted before IO pronoun.  
   **Correct Spanish:** `A Maria se le perdio el pasaporte.`

9. **Mistake:** `Acabamos el tiempo`  
   **Why it happens:** Run-out event is expressed as direct transitive action.  
   **Correct Spanish:** `Se nos acabo el tiempo.`

10. **Mistake:** `Me cai el telefono`  
    **Why it happens:** IO pattern for thing dropping is confused with person falling (me cai).  
    **Correct Spanish:** `Se me cayo el telefono.`
