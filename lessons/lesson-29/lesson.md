# Spanish Daily Lesson 29: HABER for "Have," "There Is," and "Hay Que"

## 1) cognitive_shift

**English trap**  
Using one English "have/there is" logic and trying to map `haber` to possession.

**Spanish logic**  
`Haber` is a grammar tool, not possession (`tener` is possession).  
Use it in three high-frequency frames:
- auxiliary perfect tenses (`he comido`, `habia visto`)
- impersonal existence (`hay`, `habia`, `hubo`, `habra`)
- impersonal obligation (`hay que + infinitive`)

**target pattern / formula**  
- Present perfect: `haber + participio`  
- Past perfect: `habia + participio`  
- Existence: `hay / habia / hubo / habra`  
- General obligation: `hay que + infinitive`  
- `hay` works for singular and plural nouns

**5 natural examples**  
1. Has visto ese correo?  
2. Habiamos terminado antes de la reunion.  
3. Hay muchas personas en la sala.  
4. Hubo un accidente anoche.  
5. Hay que revisar los datos hoy.

## 3) pattern_mutation

Transform each base sentence as instructed.

1. Base: He comido. -> change subject to `nosotros`.  
2. Base: Han escrito una carta. -> change direct object to plural `cartas`.  
3. Base: Ella ha abierto la ventana. -> make it negative.  
4. Base: Yo he puesto los papeles en el escritorio. -> change subject to `usted`.  
5. Base: Has visto la pelicula? -> change to statement with `yo`.  
6. Base: Hay una silla disponible. -> change noun to plural `sillas`.  
7. Base: Hay mucho trafico en la ciudad. -> change to past incomplete background.  
8. Base: Habia muchos carros en el estacionamiento. -> change to preterite event form.  
9. Base: Hubo un concierto en el parque. -> change to future.  
10. Base: No hay cafe. -> change to affirmative with `te`.  
11. Base: Yo habia estudiado antes del examen. -> change subject to `ellos`.  
12. Base: Ella habia terminado la tarea. -> make it question form.  
13. Base: Hay que estudiar para aprender. -> change infinitive to `escuchar`.  
14. Base: Hay que cortar el apio. -> make it negative recommendation.  
15. Base: Habra un concierto el viernes. -> change weekday to `sabado`.

## 4) contrastive_discrimination

Choose the correct Spanish form.

1. Have you eaten? (`Has comido?` vs `Tienes comido?`)  
2. We have seen that movie. (`Hemos visto esa pelicula.` vs `Somos visto esa pelicula.`)  
3. There are people here. (`Hay personas aqui.` vs `Estan personas aqui.`)  
4. There was an accident. (`Hubo un accidente.` vs `Habia un accidente.` as completed event)  
5. There was a lot of noise, background. (`Habia mucho ruido.` vs `Hubo mucho ruido.` in background context)  
6. There will be a concert. (`Habra un concierto.` vs `Tendra un concierto.`)  
7. One has to practice. (`Hay que practicar.` vs `Es que practicar.`)  
8. They had prepared everything. (`Habian preparado todo.` vs `Tuvieron preparado todo.`)  
9. Is there milk? (`Hay leche?` vs `Esta leche?`)  
10. I have done the task. (`He hecho la tarea.` vs `Tengo hecho la tarea.`)

## 7) common_errors

1. **Mistake:** `Tienes comido?`  
   **Why it happens:** Confusing possession `tener` with auxiliary `haber`.  
   **Correct Spanish:** `Has comido?`

2. **Mistake:** `Hayn personas`  
   **Why it happens:** Trying to pluralize `hay`.  
   **Correct Spanish:** `Hay personas.`

3. **Mistake:** `Haber` used for possession  
   **Why it happens:** Direct translation of English have.  
   **Correct Spanish:** Possession uses `tener`; auxiliary uses `haber`.

4. **Mistake:** `Hubo mucho ruido` for background descriptions  
   **Why it happens:** Not distinguishing event vs background framing.  
   **Correct Spanish:** `Habia mucho ruido` for background.

5. **Mistake:** `Habia un accidente` when reporting a completed incident  
   **Why it happens:** Overusing imperfect.  
   **Correct Spanish:** `Hubo un accidente.`

6. **Mistake:** `Hay que estudias`  
   **Why it happens:** Conjugating verb after impersonal frame.  
   **Correct Spanish:** `Hay que estudiar.`

7. **Mistake:** Participio agreement (`he comidas`)  
   **Why it happens:** Treating participle as adjective.  
   **Correct Spanish:** In perfect tenses participle stays fixed (`he comido`).

8. **Mistake:** Missing irregular participles (`he escribido`)  
   **Why it happens:** Applying regular endings universally.  
   **Correct Spanish:** `he escrito`, `he visto`, `he hecho`, etc.

9. **Mistake:** `No esta cafe` for there is no coffee  
   **Why it happens:** Using location verb instead of existence frame.  
   **Correct Spanish:** `No hay cafe.`

10. **Mistake:** Avoiding `hay que` and forcing subject  
    **Why it happens:** Literal "you have to" translation habit.  
    **Correct Spanish:** use impersonal obligation chunk `hay que + infinitive`.
