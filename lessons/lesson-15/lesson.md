# Spanish Daily Lesson 15 (Writing-First)

## 1) cognitive_shift

- **English trap:** Repeating full nouns every sentence and translating object position word-for-word.
- **Spanish logic:** Replace direct objects with pronouns (`lo/la/los/las`) and place them either before the conjugated verb or attached to an infinitive.
- **target pattern/formula:**
  - `Pronoun + conjugated verb` (e.g., `Lo veo.`)
  - `conjugated verb + infinitive` -> pronoun before conjugated verb **or** attached to infinitive
  - `lo` (neuter) for ideas/facts: `No lo sé.`

### 5 natural examples
1. ¿Conoces a Juan? Sí, lo conozco.
2. ¿Quieres ver la película? Sí, quiero verla.
3. ¿Ves las llaves? No, no las veo.
4. ¿Quieres conocer a María? Sí, la quiero conocer.
5. ¿Qué quieres hacer? No lo sé.

## 7) common_errors

1. **Putting object pronoun after conjugated verb**  
   Why: English object order transfer.  
   Correct: `Lo veo.`
2. **Using wrong gender (`lo` vs `la`)**  
   Why: English “it” has no gender.  
   Correct: `La película` -> `la`
3. **Using wrong number (`lo/la` vs `los/las`)**  
   Why: Pronoun agreement not tracked.  
   Correct: `Los documentos` -> `los`
4. **Using `lo` for female person**  
   Why: Overgeneralizing default pronoun.  
   Correct: `¿Conoces a María? Sí, la conozco.`
5. **Using `conocer` pronouns for facts**  
   Why: Saber/conocer meanings blend.  
   Correct: `No lo sé.` (for idea/fact)
6. **Using `la sé` for general “I don’t know”**  
   Why: Literal noun omitted but gender forced.  
   Better: `No lo sé.`
7. **Forgetting personal `a` in full-noun sentence**  
   Why: English has no equivalent marker.  
   Correct: `Conozco a Juan.`
8. **Attaching pronoun to conjugated verb**  
   Why: Confusing with infinitive attachment.  
   Correct: `Lo quiero ver` / `Quiero verlo`
9. **Accent missing when pronoun attached (advanced writing issue)**  
   Why: Stress changes with enclitics in some forms.  
   Correct: watch accent rules in forms like `dámelo` (later topic)
10. **Using `mirar` exclusively for films in this course frame**  
    Why: Direct translation from “watch.”  
    Preferred here: `ver` (`Quiero verla.`)
