# Spanish Daily Lesson 17 (Writing-First)

## 1) cognitive_shift

- **English trap:** Using one “like/love” verb for all situations.
- **Spanish logic:** Spanish splits emotional meaning by pattern: `gustar/encantar/fascinar` for things/activities, `caer bien/mal` for non-romantic interpersonal vibe, and `querer/amar` for love.
- **target pattern/formula:**
  - `me/te/le/nos/les + gusta(n)/encanta(n)/fascina(n) + thing/activity`
  - `a X + le cae bien/mal + person`
  - `querer/amar + a + person/pet`

### 5 natural examples
1. Me encanta viajar.
2. A Ana le gustan las fresas.
3. No me cae bien mi jefe.
4. Quiero a mi familia.
5. ¿Te fascina la música clásica?

## 3) pattern_mutation

Transform each base sentence as instructed.

1. Base: `Me gusta el chocolate.` -> make it stronger with `encantar`.
2. Base: `Nos encanta viajar.` -> make it negative.
3. Base: `A María le gustan las flores.` -> change to singular noun.
4. Base: `A Juan le cae bien María.` -> make it negative.
5. Base: `No me cae bien mi jefe.` -> make it affirmative.
6. Base: `A Lucía le caen bien sus compañeros.` -> change to singular person.
7. Base: `Amo a mi familia.` -> rewrite with `querer`.
8. Base: `Te quiero.` -> rewrite with `amar`.
9. Base: `Me gustan los perros.` -> change to activity (`correr`).
10. Base: `A ellos les encanta el teatro.` -> change to plural thing (`las películas`).
11. Base: `No nos cae bien el gerente.` -> change to plural managers.
12. Base: `Carlos ama a Ana.` -> turn into question.
13. Base: `Me fascina la cultura japonesa.` -> change to plural cultures.
14. Base: `Les gustan los videojuegos.` -> change to singular game.
15. Base: `Juan y yo nos queremos.` -> rewrite with `amar`.

## 4) contrastive_discrimination

Choose the most natural pattern and write in Spanish.

1. “I like coffee.” (`me gusta` vs `yo gusto`)
2. “I love coffee.” (`me encanta` vs `amo el café`) daily preference context.
3. “I like Juan” (non-romantic). (`me cae bien Juan` vs `me gusta Juan`)
4. “I like Juan” (romantic). (`me gusta Juan` vs `me cae bien Juan`)
5. “I love my parents.” (`amo/quiero a mis padres` vs `me encantan mis padres`) familial love.
6. “They like to travel.” (`les gusta viajar` vs `les gustan viajar`)
7. “My boss doesn’t like me (non-romantic).” (`a mi jefe no le caigo bien` vs `no le gusto`)
8. “I’m fascinated by this topic.” (`me fascina este tema` vs `yo fascino este tema`)
9. “We don’t get along with the owner.” (`no nos cae bien el dueño` vs `no nos gusta el dueño`) non-romantic interpersonal.
10. “I love you.” (`te amo / te quiero` vs `me encantas`) direct love statement.

## 7) common_errors

1. **Using `yo gusto` for liking things**  
   Why: Direct subject mapping from English.  
   Correct: `Me gusta...`
2. **Using `gustan` with infinitive verbs**  
   Why: Infinitives mistaken as plural nouns.  
   Correct: `Me encanta viajar.`
3. **Using `me gusta Juan` for non-romantic contexts**  
   Why: English “I like Juan” ambiguity.  
   Better non-romantic: `Juan me cae bien.`
4. **Using `caer bien` for romantic love**  
   Why: All “like” meanings merged.  
   Correct romantic: `Me gusta Juan.`
5. **Forgetting personal `a` with `querer/amar` + people/pets**  
   Why: English has no marker.  
   Correct: `Quiero a mi perro.`
6. **Using `amo` for everyday mild preference**  
   Why: Over-literal “I love.”  
   Natural preference: `Me encanta el café.`
7. **Wrong indirect object pronoun with `caer bien/mal`**  
   Why: Subject vs experiencer confusion.  
   Correct: `A mí me cae bien...`
8. **Using direct-object pronouns with gustar-type verbs**  
   Why: Pronoun systems mixed.  
   Correct: `Le encanta`, not `La encanta`.
9. **Dropping article in gustar noun phrases**  
   Why: English article omission transfer.  
   Correct: `Me gusta el chocolate.`
10. **Using plain `amar` without object preposition**  
    Why: English structure copied.  
    Correct: `Amo a mi familia.`
