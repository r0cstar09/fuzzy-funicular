# Spanish Daily Lesson 73: Adjective Position and Meaning Shifts

## 1) cognitive_shift

**English trap**  
Always putting adjectives after the noun in Spanish because that was the first classroom rule.

**Spanish logic**  
Position is a meaning signal. After the noun = objective quality. Before the noun = subjective view, emphasis, or a different sense of the same adjective (nuevo, viejo, pobre, grande/gran, mismo, bueno/buen, malo/mal).

**target pattern / formula**  
- restrictive/objective: `noun + adjective` (casa grande, coche nuevo)  
- subjective/emphasis: `adjective + noun` (hermosa ciudad, deliciosa cena)  
- meaning shift before: nuevo/viejo/antiguo/pobre/gran/mismo/unico/simple/diferente  
- bueno/malo before masculine singular: `buen medico / mal resultado`  
- fixed pre-noun: `mucho/poco/ambos + noun`; `primero/segundo/tercero + noun`

**5 natural examples**  
1. Compre un nuevo coche, pero no es un coche nuevo.  
2. Es un viejo amigo, no un amigo viejo.  
3. Es un buen abogado, pero no un abogado bueno en lo personal.  
4. Tenemos poco tiempo y mucho trabajo.  
5. Vivimos en la misma calle, pero en casas diferentes.

## 7) common_errors

1. **Mistake:** `Compre un coche nuevo` (meaning new to me, but brand-new intended)  
   **Why it happens:** Nuevo after noun is read as physically brand-new; before-noun sense is not applied.  
   **Correct Spanish:** `Compre un nuevo coche.`

2. **Mistake:** `Es un amigo viejo` (meaning longtime friend)  
   **Why it happens:** Viejo after noun signals age, not long-standing relationship.  
   **Correct Spanish:** `Es un viejo amigo.`

3. **Mistake:** `Un pobre hombre` (meaning without money, but unfortunate sense intended)  
   **Why it happens:** Pobre before noun shifts to unfortunate/pitiable meaning.  
   **Correct Spanish:** `Un hombre pobre.`

4. **Mistake:** `Un gran departamento` (meaning large apartment)  
   **Why it happens:** Gran before noun signals greatness/quality, not size.  
   **Correct Spanish:** `Un departamento grande.`

5. **Mistake:** `Es un doctor bueno` (meaning skilled doctor)  
   **Why it happens:** Bueno after noun often reads as moral goodness, not professional skill.  
   **Correct Spanish:** `Es un buen doctor.`

6. **Mistake:** `Es un buen director` (meaning unethical director as a person)  
   **Why it happens:** Buen before noun is used for skill, not personal morality.  
   **Correct Spanish:** `Es un director malo.`

7. **Mistake:** `Ideas diferentes` (meaning various ideas)  
   **Why it happens:** Diferente after noun means not the same; before noun can mean various.  
   **Correct Spanish:** `Diferentes ideas.`

8. **Mistake:** `Tiempo mucho`  
   **Why it happens:** Mucho/poco are forced after the noun by default adjective rule.  
   **Correct Spanish:** `Mucho tiempo.`

9. **Mistake:** `La version primera`  
   **Why it happens:** Ordinal numbers are placed after noun like English.  
   **Correct Spanish:** `La primera version.`

10. **Mistake:** `Un adorno simple` (meaning mere decoration)  
    **Why it happens:** Simple after noun describes style; before noun can minimize to only/mere.  
    **Correct Spanish:** `Un simple adorno.`
