# Spanish Daily Lesson 16 (Writing-First)

## 1) cognitive_shift

- **English trap:** Translating “I like X” as “Yo gusto X.”
- **Spanish logic:** In Spanish, the thing liked is the subject. You express “to me / to you / to them” with indirect object pronouns.
- **target pattern/formula:**
  - `me/te/le/nos/les + gusta + singular noun or infinitive`
  - `me/te/le/nos/les + gustan + plural noun`
  - Optional clarifier: `A Juan le gusta...`

### 5 natural examples
1. Me gusta el café.
2. ¿Te gusta bailar?
3. A María le gustan las flores.
4. Nos gusta viajar.
5. ¿Les gusta la música a ellos?

## 3) pattern_mutation

Transform each base sentence as instructed.

1. Base: `Me gusta el café.` -> change noun to plural (`los cafés`).
2. Base: `¿Te gusta bailar?` -> change infinitive to `correr`.
3. Base: `Le gustan las flores a María.` -> make it singular noun (`la flor`).
4. Base: `Nos gusta viajar.` -> make it negative.
5. Base: `¿Les gusta el teatro?` -> change to plural subject thing (`los teatros`).
6. Base: `A Juan le gusta leer.` -> turn into question.
7. Base: `Me gustan las películas.` -> change to singular (`la película`).
8. Base: `¿Te gusta el invierno?` -> answer no in full sentence.
9. Base: `A ellos les gustan los animales.` -> change to feminine plural noun (`las plantas`).
10. Base: `Le gusta el mar a Laura.` -> change to `nosotros` and `la playa`.
11. Base: `Nos gustan las verduras.` -> change to infinitive pattern (`comer verduras`).
12. Base: `¿Le gusta cantar a María?` -> change verb to `bailar`.
13. Base: `Me gusta escribir.` -> add another infinitive in same sentence.
14. Base: `¿Te gustan las flores?` -> rewrite with clarifier for Juan.
15. Base: `Les gusta la música.` -> rewrite with explicit subject `A mis amigos`.

## 4) contrastive_discrimination

Choose the most natural option and write in Spanish.

1. “I like coffee.” (`me gusta` vs `yo gusto`)
2. “I like books.” (`me gustan los libros` vs `me gusta los libros`)
3. “I like to dance.” (`me gusta bailar` vs `me gustan bailar`)
4. “Do you like chocolate?” (`¿te gusta el chocolate?` vs `¿te gustan el chocolate?`)
5. “We like vegetables.” (`nos gustan las verduras` vs `nos gusta las verduras`)
6. “Juan likes music.” (`a Juan le gusta la música` vs `Juan gusta la música`)
7. “They like to travel.” (`les gusta viajar` vs `les gustan viajar`)
8. “Do Ana and Juan like movies?” (`¿a Ana y a Juan les gustan las películas?` vs `¿a Ana y a Juan le gustan...?`)
9. “I don’t like that.” (`no me gusta eso` vs `no gusto eso`)
10. “Do you like me?” (`¿te gusto?` vs `¿te gusta yo?`) person-liking structure.

## 7) common_errors

1. **Using `yo gusto` for “I like”**  
   Why: Direct English mapping.  
   Correct: `Me gusta...`
2. **Using `gusta` with plural nouns**  
   Why: Verb agreement ignored.  
   Correct: `Me gustan los libros.`
3. **Using `gustan` with infinitive**  
   Why: Infinitive treated as plural.  
   Correct: `Me gusta bailar.`
4. **Forgetting indirect object pronoun (`me/te/le...`)**  
   Why: English does not require it.  
   Correct: `Le gusta la música.`
5. **Wrong pronoun for person (`le` vs `les`)**  
   Why: Singular/plural recipient confusion.  
   Correct: `A Juan le gusta...` / `A Juan y Ana les gusta...`
6. **Dropping article with noun after gustar**  
   Why: English article habits.  
   Correct: `Me gusta el café.`
7. **Overusing clarifier every sentence unnaturally**  
   Why: Fear of ambiguity.  
   Natural: pronoun alone when context is clear.
8. **Using subject pronouns as if they drive gustar**  
   Why: “I/you” treated as subject of liking.  
   Correct: `Nos gusta la música.` not `Nosotros gustamos...`
9. **Wrong question order copied from English**  
   Why: Literal transfer.  
   Correct: `¿Te gusta... ?`
10. **Confusing “I like you” with normal gustar noun pattern**  
    Why: Person as direct subject creates different meaning.  
    Correct: `Me gustas tú.` / `¿Te gusto?`
