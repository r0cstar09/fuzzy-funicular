# Spanish Daily Lesson 50: Non-Specific Relative Clauses (Subjunctive)

## 1) cognitive_shift

**English trap**  
Using the same verb form after "that/who" whether the thing is specific or just desired/unknown.

**Spanish logic**  
In Spanish, when you describe a **non-specific** item or person with `que`, use subjunctive in that clause.  
When the item/person is **specific and identified**, use indicative.

**target pattern / formula**  
- non-specific: `quiero/busco/necesito/prefiero + noun + que + subjuntivo`  
- specific: `quiero/busco... + el/la/al... + noun + que + indicativo`  
- common non-specific starters:
  - `busco a alguien que sepa...`
  - `busco a alguien que pueda...`

**5 natural examples**  
1. Quiero una silla que sea comoda.  
2. Quiero la silla que es comoda.  
3. Busco una casa que de al parque.  
4. Busco la casa que da al parque.  
5. Busco a alguien que pueda ayudarme.

## 7) common_errors

1. **Mistake:** using indicative with non-specific noun  
   **Why it happens:** direct translation from English relative clause usage.  
   **Correct Spanish:** non-specific reference triggers subjunctive.

2. **Mistake:** using subjunctive with clearly identified noun  
   **Why it happens:** overgeneralizing subjunctive rule.  
   **Correct Spanish:** specific identified noun uses indicative.

3. **Mistake:** ignoring article contrast (`una` vs `la`, `un` vs `el`)  
   **Why it happens:** missing specificity signal.  
   **Correct Spanish:** article choice often guides mood choice.

4. **Mistake:** `busco alguien que...` without personal `a` in natural speech contexts  
   **Why it happens:** uncertainty about personal `a`.  
   **Correct Spanish:** `busco a alguien que...` is standard and natural.

5. **Mistake:** `busco al ... que` with non-specific meaning  
   **Why it happens:** accidental specificity from contraction `al`.  
   **Correct Spanish:** non-specific search generally keeps indefinite article.

6. **Mistake:** leaving verb in infinitive after `que`  
   **Why it happens:** focusing on meaning and skipping conjugation.  
   **Correct Spanish:** conjugate subjunctive/indicative normally.

7. **Mistake:** mood chosen without checking referent status  
   **Why it happens:** grammar-first instead of meaning-first processing.  
   **Correct Spanish:** ask: "Do I know exactly which one?"

8. **Mistake:** translating "I am looking for the..." as non-specific  
   **Why it happens:** English "the" not weighed strongly.  
   **Correct Spanish:** specific with `el/la/al` tends toward indicative.

9. **Mistake:** applying rule to all relative clauses, even factual known references  
   **Why it happens:** simplification strategy.  
   **Correct Spanish:** subjunctive here is tied to indefiniteness/unknown existence.

10. **Mistake:** avoiding this pattern due to fear of subjunctive  
    **Why it happens:** overthinking mood labels.  
    **Correct Spanish:** make quick specificity decision then choose mood.
