# Spanish Daily Lesson 13 (Writing-First)

## 1) cognitive_shift

- **English trap:** Using `estar` for every “is/are” sentence.
- **Spanish logic:** Use `ser` for identity, origin, profession, relationships, and stable characteristics.
- **target pattern/formula:**
  - `Sujeto + ser + identidad/rol`
  - `Sujeto + ser + de + lugar` (origin)
  - `Sujeto + ser + adjetivo de rasgo`

### 5 natural examples
1. Soy de Colombia.
2. Ella es ingeniera.
3. Ellos son mis compañeros de trabajo.
4. Mi jefe es muy organizado.
5. ¿Quién es esa mujer?

## 3) pattern_mutation

Transform each base sentence as instructed.

1. Base: `Soy estudiante.` -> change to plural (`nosotros`).
2. Base: `Ella es profesora.` -> turn into question.
3. Base: `Somos de México.` -> change country to `Colombia`.
4. Base: `Ellos son inteligentes.` -> change to feminine plural.
5. Base: `Mi casa es grande.` -> make it negative.
6. Base: `La película es interesante.` -> change adjective to `aburrida`.
7. Base: `Juan es carpintero.` -> add adjective `bueno` correctly.
8. Base: `María es profesora.` -> add adjective `excelente`.
9. Base: `¿De dónde eres?` -> answer with `Canada`.
10. Base: `¿Quién es ella?` -> answer with `my friend`.
11. Base: `El hombre es alto.` -> change subject to plural.
12. Base: `La mujer es amable.` -> make it very kind.
13. Base: `Nosotros somos compañeros de clase.` -> change to `they`.
14. Base: `El libro es aburrido.` -> change to plural books.
15. Base: `Soy de Estados Unidos.` -> change to `tú` question.

## 4) contrastive_discrimination

Choose the most natural pattern in Spanish.

1. “I am tired right now.” (`estoy` vs `soy`)
2. “I am a patient person.” (`soy` vs `estoy`)
3. “She is from Peru.” (`es de` vs `está de`)
4. “The soup is hot (right now).” (`está` vs `es`)
5. “The movie is boring (in general).” (`es aburrida` vs `está aburrida`)
6. “Who is he?” (`¿quién es...?` vs `¿quién está...?`)
7. “They are my friends.” (`son` vs `están`) identity relationship.
8. “The office is closed.” (`está cerrada` vs `es cerrada`) temporary condition.
9. “The car is red.” (`es rojo` vs `está rojo`) color trait.
10. “The banana is green (unripe).” (`está verde` vs `es verde`) condition reading.

## 7) common_errors

1. **Using `estar` for profession**  
   Why: One-verb transfer from English “to be.”  
   Correct: `Soy profesor.`
2. **Using article with profession in neutral statements**  
   Why: English requires “a/an.”  
   Correct: `Ella es ingeniera.`
3. **Forgetting article when adding adjective to profession**  
   Why: Rule changes with adjective not internalized.  
   Correct: `Es una buena ingeniera.`
4. **Using `estar de` for origin**  
   Why: Literal “from” mapping confusion.  
   Correct: `Soy de México.`
5. **Using `ser` for temporary condition**  
   Why: Traits vs states not separated.  
   Correct: `Estoy cansado.`
6. **Using `estar` for identity relation**  
   Why: “is my friend” translated with state verb.  
   Correct: `Es mi amigo.`
7. **Wrong adjective agreement after `ser`**  
   Why: English adjective invariance.  
   Correct: `Las mujeres son inteligentes.`
8. **Mixing `quién` and `quiénes`**  
   Why: Singular/plural question mismatch.  
   Correct: `¿Quién es ella?` / `¿Quiénes son ellos?`
9. **Dropping accent in interrogatives**  
   Why: Fast typing.  
   Correct: `¿Quién?`, `¿De dónde?`
10. **Using `estar` for inherent color descriptions**  
    Why: Overextension from temporary conditions.  
    Correct: `El coche es rojo.`
